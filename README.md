# Data visualiser

Visualising and interpreting your survey or interview data.

[Open the student workspace](https://stefancouperus.github.io/data-visualiser/). Bring your own files or reopen a downloaded project. The visualisation workspace runs in the browser. Students enter their workshop code when using the Data bot.

## Use the workspace

1. **Upload.** Choose the Qualtrics or interview shortcut, or bring a CSV/TSV, Excel workbook, Word document or text file. Choose an Excel sheet and its header row. In Qualtrics, export answer labels and retain the original headers.
2. **Check.** Inspect field types, ordered categories, units, blank answers and exclusions. Combine split select-all fields when needed. If every indicator is blank, the combined question counts as skipped. An explicit row of zeros counts as an answer with no selections. Filtering to one option includes everyone who ticked it, including people who ticked other options. A matching Qualtrics questionnaire carries existing record exclusions into the labelled dataset. For Word, map comment or colour markings to codes, or choose delimiters for paragraph-level inline labels. For a coded spreadsheet, identify source, excerpt and code columns. Repeated code-assignment rows require a passage identifier to count the excerpt once. Taguette users can import English all-highlights DOCX exports or map spreadsheet columns. Confirm the complete source list when highlights omit uncoded documents.
3. **Explore.** Choose a question, compare groups, inspect counts and percentages or fit a guided regression. Read linked survey answers through the record view. From Check, convert an open-answer column into a collection retaining respondent attributes. For interviews, use **Upload → Add participant information** to import the participant table, then link it to the interviewees. Review source IDs and unmatched records.
4. **Compare.** Put two views alongside each other and choose whether to link filters. Coded passages, sources and respondents have distinct counting units. Open the underlying evidence before interpreting differences.
5. **Save and export.** The app autosaves your project in this browser. Use **Home → Projects on this device** to resume it, including after Back, a refresh or closing the tab. Choose **Download project with data** to get one ZIP containing the editable project and its Word report. Reopen the ZIP directly with **Open a saved project**. Older JSON projects still open. Home lists each device copy with its last save date and time, even if you cancel a download. Requesting a full backup removes that version from the in-workspace recent list until you edit it again. Settings-only downloads require matching imports and omit passage text and source data. A readable HTML report opens offline and can be printed. Export either panel or the pair as SVG or PNG, or copy a figure.

The sidebar identifies the project method beneath **Upload**. Click the logo to go Home and open a different project. Written responses stay inside their survey project. Participant information can be linked to interviews within an interview project.

**View project log** shows a dated record of completed steps, saved evidence and findings. You can add a note in your own words and download the log separately with **Download Word report**. For an older project, the log identifies when recording began.

Explore offers question-based choices. Compare opens two labelled figures with independent editing controls and retains the right-hand view when you revisit it. The Data bot can use the left figure, the right figure or both. Use its shortcuts to explain the current pattern, draw a different chart of the same selection or draft a caption for review.

For surveys, choose **Explore → Compare questions by group**. Tick up to six questions and choose the question that defines the groups. The heatmap puts the groups across the top and lists each question’s answer options down the side. **How answers differ between groups** shows the share of each group that chose an answer. **Which groups gave each answer** shows how the people who chose that answer are distributed across groups. **Group mix** draws one horizontal bar per answer, with a segment for each group. **Data table** shows the count and denominator for every cell. Select a cell to read its matching responses, or choose **Spot patterns** for observations and a suggested visual. Use **Which answers people gave together** to explore combinations within individual responses.

To combine survey answers, name the choices in chat or use **Chat tools → Combine answers** to pick them. For each selected question the app counts respondents who chose at least one of those answers, then shows the count, the number who answered and the percentage. The calculation follows the current filter, project exclusions and grouping.

For coded interviews, choose **Explore themes → Compare themes**. Tick up to six codes from your project. **Individual interviews** shows how often each theme was coded in each transcript. Switch to passage percentages to account for different numbers of included passages. To compare groups, choose a participant detail from your project, or use **Add interview groups** to enter one. Count each interview once to see how widespread a theme is, or count passages to see how much of the included material carries that code. Choose **Share of each theme’s passages** to draw one horizontal bar per theme, divided among the interviews or groups that contributed the passages. Select a cell, bar or dot to read its matching original passages. The passage number opens the full transcript at that point.

With one interview in the comparison, **Spot patterns** reports which selected themes recur and which pairs were coded in the same original passage. **Read shared passages** lets you choose a pair and open the transcript to see how its two codes relate. With several interviews, **Spot patterns** compares the interviews or groups. In either case, the bot can suggest a figure for you to accept.

In Explore or Compare, choose **Figure style** above a chart to preview Workshop, Research report, Policy brief or Presentation on your data. Each panel has its own style and colour palette. Choose the style’s default colours, a named palette or greyscale. Policy brief can highlight a category or group where the chart supports it. Figure exports and saved projects keep these choices.

Use **Download project with data** for a portable ZIP. On the start page, choose **Open a saved project** or drop the file there. The full project includes a dataset-specific bot activity record. Tick **Include full Data bot conversations** to save the questions and replies as well. Settings-only exports omit both. Older project downloads still open.

**Opening the Data bot.** Chat and bot-assisted tasks share a session in the same browser tab. If a task asks for the workshop course code, choose **Confirm code** to continue directly with that task. You can enter labels by hand and review saved suggestions without signing in. The unlocked panel opens with an optional research question or puzzle above the chat. Save it with the project, skip it for now, or start chatting immediately. **Help settings** lets you choose Contextual hints (the default), Help on request or Guided exploration. Hover or focus an option, or tap its question mark, for an explanation. The browser remembers your choice. **Chat tools** contains the current selection, extra actions and project activity.

**Data bot.** The Worker sends the current summary and selected evidence to the university gateway, using Gemma as primary and Qwen as fallback. Review the context before adding whole sources. For suggested coding, select up to ten passages and approve or dismiss each grounded suggestion. Ordinary visualisation does not contact the gateway. Full project downloads include data and should be shared accordingly.

**Interview coding.** Choose **Upload uncoded transcripts** or **Upload coded interviews**. Each interview has a transcript and its own review status. To work deductively, start under **Set up your codebook**. Choose **Enter labels manually** to build your own codebook. Use **Add label** for a standalone label, or **Add category** followed by **Add label to this category**. The same sublabel can appear under different questions. Its category identifies it when you or the bot code a passage. To start from a file, choose **Extract labels from a codebook** and upload a DOCX, text-based PDF, TXT, XLSX, CSV or TSV file. Check the editable draft and choose **Use these labels**. Its status and labels are visible in the workspace. Closing the editor keeps the draft. You can reopen it to finish your review. A flat codebook gives one row per label. In a grouped codebook, open a question or category to review each subcode, its definition and any inclusion or exclusion rules. A repeated answer such as ‘Monthly’ keeps its question when it also occurs elsewhere. Word lists and clearly labelled tables are read locally. The bot interprets sections that need interpretation, and you review the resulting draft before applying it.

Use **Choose passages for coding** on a transcript to select the material for manual coding, bot runs, review and figures. Recognised interviewer turns and consent exchanges start unticked. If the transcript uses initials or numbered speakers, choose the interviewer from the speaker list. Before sending a coding request, you can preview the preceding questions that will accompany the selected answers or untick that option. Only the selected answers can supply supporting quotations. You can change the selection and reopen the original text. During a bot run you can narrow its selection further. Each label starts with its own colour. **Manage labels** changes its colour or assigns it to a category. **Manage categories** creates, renames and removes categories.

Under **Apply your labels**, choose **Code manually** to label a passage or highlighted quotation. Tick the approved labels that fit and save. **Code with the bot** lets you choose the current interview, several named interviews or the entire collection. Choose **Send to bot for coding** to start. The bot matches passages to the approved codebook. A passage may have no matching label. Choose **Review suggested coding**, inspect the quotations and tick the assignments to keep. **Apply reviewed coding** saves them to the transcripts. You can return to a labelled quotation to correct it.

Choose **Check for missed labels** for a second reading with your approved codebook. Select one, several or all interviews and confirm which passages to check. The bot revisits both coded and uncoded passages. A separate evidence check reads each proposed label against the answer and its codebook definition. Either reading can return no suggestions. In **Review suggested coding**, tick the additions you want to keep after checking their quotations. **Finish review** saves those decisions, including a decision to accept none. Closing the review leaves the draft available to resume.

For inductive coding, ask the bot to suggest labels from named interviews, or choose the interviews and passages yourself. Confirm the selection to start. **Review draft labels** reopens the suggestions under **Set up your codebook**. Check the supporting quotations, edit the names and meanings, and keep the labels that fit. **Use these labels** approves the codebook. To code the full collection, choose **Code with the bot** and select all interviews. You can close the review and return to the saved draft before approval. You can also start with labels from an already coded interview. Use **Manage labels** to edit the project vocabulary. Codebook changes that affect earlier decisions make those interviews available for checking again.

After a refresh, choose **Resume coding project** in **Projects on this device** on Home. Use **Choose passages for coding** to check which parts of each interview enter the coding review. **Manage labels** lets you change individual colours and organise related labels into named categories. **Save coding project** downloads the transcripts, codebook, draft labels, accepted coding and any pending coding review. Use **+ Add interview** to extend the collection with files or a transcript folder. Download a coded Word document for one interview or a ZIP with a Word document for each interview. Each document carries its own colour legend. Once each interview is marked as checked, **Coding complete · explore themes** opens the visualisation workspace. A highlights-only export covers the supplied excerpts. Theme charts count each original imported passage once. Repeating the same label on several quotations in a passage contributes one match. Two different labels on different words of that passage count as appearing together there. Counting by interview instead gives each listed interview one unit.

## Reading and exporting figures

Use **Edit visual** beside each figure to edit chart wording or reorder categories and series. These edits travel with a saved visual and exported figures.
In **Explore**, choose **Compare groups** and two single-answer survey questions to see a **Gap chart** when two groups have usable answers. Each row joins the percentages for one answer in each group. For a numerical answer, choose **Box plot** to see its median, middle half and full range, either overall or by group.

A multiple-answer survey question can be the outcome or a filter. To form groups, choose a single-answer question or an individual checkbox indicator column. Each filtered figure carries the field name and the selected value into its export, including when the two panels in **Compare** use different filters.

When a chart counts passages, a theme counts once per original passage. Highlighting several quotations with the same label and accepting the same label from the bot give that passage the same weight. To count how widely a theme occurs across interviews, choose **Interview** as the counting unit.

In an interview or written-answer collection, choose **Count words in passages**. Select all passages or one theme. Use **Chart settings** to filter by source or change which common words are omitted. Select a word to read its passages. The ranked table shows how often each word occurs and how many passages and sources contain it.

## Development and deployment

Use Node 22. Run `npm ci`, `npm run dev`, `npm test` and `npm run build`. Run the interaction checks with `npx playwright test`; install the Playwright Chromium browser on systems without the configured macOS Chrome. Browser test fixtures are synthetic and are not included in the landing page.

GitHub Actions builds the static site and deploys it to Pages from main. Set the GitHub repository variable `DATA_BOT_URL` to the HTTPS URL of the deployed Worker. Set GitHub Pages' build source to GitHub Actions. For local development, supply `VITE_BOT_URL` when starting Vite and use a local Worker with the local origin.

The relay lives in `worker/`. Its allowed web origin, rate limits and name are in `worker/wrangler.toml`. Deploy using `npx wrangler deploy --config worker/wrangler.toml`. Store `RUG_API_KEY`, `COURSE_CODE` and `SESSION_SECRET` as Cloudflare Worker secrets. Keep credentials outside Git and static build variables. Rotating the session secret invalidates existing twelve-hour sessions. Students can re-enter the same course code across workshops.

Edit `worker/data-bot.md` for shared instructions, and `worker/skills/` for task-specific teaching guidance. Redeploy the Worker after changing these files. Browser calculations supply figures and model output. Models suggest interpretations and changes for students to review.

In Explore, **Look across several questions** compares up to six survey items, either as answer combinations from the same record or as percentages for each question. It supports choice questions, multiple-answer questions and numerical questions with up to 20 observed values. Use **Look across several themes** for coded interviews to compare counts by interview or find combinations within a passage or interview. Click a chart row to inspect its evidence. The chart buttons beside the live figure change its presentation and retain the selection. These options also work in Compare, saved projects and figure exports.

In a pattern view, **Compare by group** places the same answers or themes alongside one another for each group. Survey grouping uses a separate single-answer or numeric column with up to 30 observed values. For transcripts, link interview information in Check or use **Add interview groups** to enter a group once per interview. When students ask to split a pattern by one of their project’s grouping fields, the bot keeps the ticked questions or themes and the current filter. Shares use the relevant denominator within each group and the figure reports missing group information.

With a saved research question, **Suggest what to explore** recommends available measures and explains their relevance. **Suggest groups to compare** looks for a recorded grouping that fits the question and preserves the selected items. Students review the proposed chart before drawing it.

Without a saved focus, **Suggest groups to compare** checks the current selection locally and offers a chart for review. It considers up to 12 available grouping fields with 2–12 observed categories, shortlists by completeness and the size of the second-largest group, retaining dataset order for ties, and skips identifiers, copies of selected responses and contrasts based on a single record or interview. It ranks observed differences with a preference for fuller coverage and larger groups. These are exploratory comparisons. Students can accept, decline or change the selection.
